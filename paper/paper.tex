\begin{abstract}
Most music recommendation systems keep feeding the popular while starving
emerging artists, because traditional algorithms chase popularity signals and
largely ignore content affinity. This paper presents EchoPulse AI, a locally
deployed multimodal recommender that tackles this visibility gap. The system
processes lyrical, acoustic, metadata and conversational data through a
Triple-Encoder Transformer, consolidating heterogeneous datasets (WASABI,
Million Song Dataset, Lyrics 1950--2019 and TalkPlayData-2) through a five-stage
local pipeline that avoids cloud dependencies entirely. The methodology relies
on a deliberately curated Master Table of 5,710 tracks with complete multimodal
coverage, designed as a controlled proof of concept that isolates the cold-start
problem. Against collaborative filtering, BERT4Rec and SASRec baselines,
EchoPulse AI reaches competitive accuracy (Hit@10\,=\,0.312) while improving
Intra-List Diversity by 45.3\% and Emerging Artist Discovery by 157.9\%; core
retrieval components run below 250\,ms on consumer hardware. The current
implementation favours English-language content and is validated offline only,
so scaling to industrial catalogues and running online user studies are the key
next steps.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Digital streaming reshaped the music business: global revenues hit USD 29.6
billion in 2024~\cite{ifpi2024report}. That expansion has not trickled down to
emerging artists, who still face a brutal visibility gap. The first author spent
14 years playing drums in bands across Colombia, and the reality on the ground
is stark: new artists simply do not get recommended. Not because their music
lacks quality, but because algorithms systematically favour established acts.

Most platforms still lean heavily on collaborative filtering: they track who
clicks what and amplify the hits. That works for global superstars and collapses
on the cold-start problem~\cite{schein2002methods,adomavicius2005toward}. A new
track with no play history is ignored, which closes a feedback loop that funnels
attention toward the same handful of songs. In Latin American scenes the
situation is worse: local artists compete with global hits and with a flood of
algorithmically generated background music.

Content-based approaches offer a way out. Looking at lyrics, acoustic
properties and metadata makes it possible to recommend what the music actually
sounds like~\cite{ricci2015recommender,aggarwal2016recommender}. The
Transformer~\cite{vaswani2017attention} maps long-range dependencies well, which
suits the structured, multi-layered nature of musical
content~\cite{baltruvsaitis2019multimodal}.

This paper introduces EchoPulse AI, a locally deployed multimodal recommender
built within the IMS Research Group at Fundación Universitaria de Popayán. Why
local? The group had a limited cloud budget and regional connectivity is
notoriously unstable. Rather than treating those constraints as a weakness, they
forced a design that prioritises efficiency, reproducibility and zero cloud
dependency. Three contributions emerge: (i) a hybrid local data architecture
that consolidates messy, heterogeneous music datasets through a five-stage
pipeline without AWS or GCP; (ii) a Triple-Encoder Transformer with specialised
modules for lyrics, audio and conversation, formalised mathematically; and
(iii) an end-to-end evaluation showing that diversity and emerging-artist
visibility can improve dramatically without tanking accuracy. Building the
Master Table was the hardest part of the project: reconciling incompatible
identifier systems, imputing massive amounts of missing data, and forcing four
datasets into one clean collection of 5,710 tracks. That data-engineering
struggle shaped the architecture described below.

Section~\ref{sec:framework} reviews the framework and hypotheses,
Section~\ref{sec:methodology} details the pipeline and model, and
Sections~\ref{sec:results}--\ref{sec:discussion} report the evaluation, its
scope and its limitations.

\section{Framework and Hypotheses}
\label{sec:framework}

The Transformer self-attention mechanism~\cite{vaswani2017attention} computes
\begin{equation}
\text{Attention}(\mat{Q},\mat{K},\mat{V})
  = \softmax\left(\frac{\mat{Q}\mat{K}^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mat{V},
\label{eq:attention}
\end{equation}
letting the model weigh which parts of a sequence matter most. This fits music
because songs have verses, choruses and bridges that attention can pick up on.
Multimodal machine learning formalises how to combine such heterogeneous
sources~\cite{baltruvsaitis2019multimodal}; cross-modal attention proved itself
in vision-language models such as ViLBERT~\cite{lu2019vilbert} and
LXMERT~\cite{tan2019lxmert}. We adapt that mechanism to music, because early
fusion struggles to align heterogeneous feature spaces and late fusion misses
deep inter-modal correlations. On the data side, the lakehouse
paradigm~\cite{armbrust2020delta} combines data-lake flexibility with
warehouse-style transactions; we adapt those principles locally with
parquet-based storage, manual versioning and schema validation, achieving
reproducibility and traceability without cloud
infrastructure~\cite{pimentel2021dataversioning}.

Prior bias-mitigation work relies on post-processing re-ranking, inverse
propensity weighting or adversarial
debiasing~\cite{kaminskas2016diversity}, which adjust the output distribution
without giving cold-start items the content representation they lack. Sequential
baselines inherit the same flaw: BERT4Rec improves accuracy through
bidirectional encoding but keeps the popularity bias of its training
data~\cite{sun2019bert4rec}, and SASRec behaves likewise with
self-attention~\cite{kang2018self}. Conversational recommenders such as TalkPlay
generate recommendations in natural language but lack structured multimodal
integration and rigorous evaluation of their generative
components~\cite{choi2025talkplaydata2,gao2021advances}.

From this we posit two hypotheses. \textbf{H1 (multimodal fusion superiority):}
fusing lyrical, acoustic and conversational modalities through cross-attention
captures user intent better than early/late fusion or unimodal baselines.
\textbf{H2 (popularity bias mitigation):} a content-based multimodal approach
inherently avoids popularity bias, significantly improving Intra-List Diversity
(ILD) and Emerging Artist Discovery (EAD). Both are formalised through a hybrid
scoring function that combines semantic, acoustic and lexical signals,
\begin{equation}
S(q,c) = \alpha \cdot \cosine(\vect{e}_q, \vect{e}_c)
       + \beta \cdot \left(1 - \frac{\|\vect{f}_q - \vect{f}_c\|_2}
       {\|\vect{f}_q\|_2 + \|\vect{f}_c\|_2}\right)
       + \gamma \cdot \frac{|T_q \cap T_c|}{|T_q \cup T_c|},
\label{eq:scoring}
\end{equation}
with $\alpha=0.4$ for semantic embeddings, $\beta=0.3$ for acoustic continuity
and $\gamma=0.3$ for lexical filtering by Jaccard similarity over token sets
$T_q$ and $T_c$.

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Five-Stage Local Pipeline}
EchoPulse AI runs on a five-stage local architecture adapted from standard MLOps
practice for strictly local deployment. Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} maps the
journey from raw heterogeneous sources to local deployment, including the
evaluation feedback loop. The stages---Data Sources, Data Lake, Data Processing,
Data Modeling and Data Product---guarantee reproducibility and traceability
through schema validation, directory-based snapshot isolation, hierarchical
$(\textit{year}, \textit{genre}, \textit{artist\_initial})$ partitioning and a
SQLite metadata catalog.

\subsection{Data Sources, Integration and Interaction Generation}
Four sources feed the system, each contributing a different modality: the WASABI
dataset for lyrics and cultural metadata~\cite{tikat2021wasabi}; the Million
Song Dataset (MSD) for 26 continuous acoustic features such as tempo, energy,
valence, danceability and loudness~\cite{bertin2011million}; Lyrics
1950--2019 for thematic and emotional metadata encoded as sentiment vectors
$\vect{s}\in\Real^{6}$~\cite{moura2020lyrics}; and TalkPlayData-2, a corpus of
about 16,000 structured dialogues that map user intentions to song
selections~\cite{choi2025talkplaydata2}.

Integration was the hardest step: MSD uses Echo Nest identifiers while WASABI
has its own system, so we matched records heuristically on the tuple
$(\textit{song\_name}, \textit{artist\_name}, \textit{release\_year})$ with a
Levenshtein distance threshold $\delta \leq 2$, reaching 84\% coverage with
validated acoustic vectors and complete lyrics. Missing values were handled by
MICE imputation~\cite{vanbuuren2011mice} and outliers flagged with the Median
Absolute Deviation at $\kappa=3.5$~\cite{leys2013mad}. The consolidated Master
Table is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D} = \{(\vect{l}_i, \vect{a}_i, \vect{m}_i, \vect{c}_i, y_i)\}_{i=1}^{N},
\qquad N = 5{,}710,
\label{eq:master}
\end{equation}
with tokenised lyrics $\vect{l}_i$, acoustic features
$\vect{a}_i \in \Real^{26}$, metadata embeddings $\vect{m}_i$, conversational
context $\vect{c}_i$ and target identifier $y_i$.

That 5,710-track table was deliberately curated as a controlled proof of concept
to isolate the effect of multimodal fusion on the cold-start problem. Deriving
implicit feedback for offline conversational evaluation is notoriously
difficult, so we mapped the structured TalkPlayData-2 dialogues onto the Master
Table: intents expressed in the dialogues were semantically matched to track
embeddings, producing a synthetic yet structurally valid interaction matrix.
This controlled setting demonstrates popularity-bias mitigation free from the
confounding factors and extreme sparsity of uncurated industrial datasets. The
baselines (collaborative filtering, BERT4Rec, SASRec) were trained on this
identical matrix with their official implementations and default hyperparameters,
to keep the comparison fair.

\subsection{Triple-Encoder Transformer}
The first module, \textbf{IntentTransformer}, is an encoder-only Transformer
($L=4$ layers, $H=8$ heads, $d_{\text{model}}=256$) that encodes conversational
intent~\cite{wolf2020transformers}. It acts as a semantic funnel, reducing a
universe of 38,343 candidate songs to a top-10 for re-ranking, and is trained
with cross-entropy and label smoothing ($\epsilon=0.1$)~\cite{mueller2019label}.

The second module, \textbf{MST-MR} (Multimodal Sequence Transformer for Music
Recommendation), fuses modalities through cross-attention, following
principles from vision-language fusion~\cite{lu2019vilbert,tan2019lxmert}. A
lyrical encoder produces $\vect{h}_l$ from tokenised lyrics and a tabular MLP
projects the 26 acoustic features into the shared space as $\vect{h}_a$; each
modality then attends to the other,
\begin{equation}
\text{CrossAttn}(\mat{Q}_l, \mat{K}_a, \mat{V}_a)
  = \softmax\left(\frac{\mat{Q}_l \mat{K}_a^\top}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right)\mat{V}_a,
\label{eq:crossattn}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\vect{h}_{\text{fusion}}
  = \LayerNorm\bigl(\vect{h}_l + \text{CrossAttn}(\vect{h}_l, \vect{h}_a,
  \vect{h}_a)\bigr),
\label{eq:fusion}
\end{equation}
and the concatenation of $\vect{h}_{\text{fusion}}$ with the conversational
vector $\vect{h}_c$ is projected to a 512-dimensional song embedding $\vect{e}_s$
used in Equation~\eqref{eq:scoring}. MST-MR behaves as a hybrid re-ranker,
combining neural similarity (40\%) with lexical Jaccard similarity (60\%).

\subsection{Local Inference}
Serving uses a two-tier, CPU-optimised stack: a FastAPI gateway for validation,
orchestration and asynchronous response formatting, and an inference engine with
dynamic batching and INT8 quantization reaching 185 inferences per second versus
73 for direct PyTorch serving~\cite{paszke2019pytorch,jacob2018quantization}.
Quantisation cut the memory footprint by 68\% at only 1.2\% accuracy
degradation, and the 98.4\% request success rate includes fallback handling for
cold starts, missing metadata and API failures.

\section{Results and Demonstration}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Experimental Setup and Metrics}
Training and validation ran on a desktop machine with an
Intel\textsuperscript{\textregistered} Core\texttrademark{} i5-7200U CPU at
2.50\,GHz, 12.0\,GB of RAM and integrated Intel HD Graphics 620, i.e.\ commodity
consumer hardware. The model was trained on the Master Table with PyTorch 2.0
using CPU acceleration~\cite{paszke2019pytorch}, the AdamW optimizer at learning
rate $\eta=10^{-4}$~\cite{loshchilov2019decoupled}, batch size $B=32$ and early
stopping with patience $p=5$. Evaluation used a temporal 80\%/10\%/10\%
train/validation/test split to prevent data leakage.

We report retrieval quality and bias-mitigation quality. \textbf{Hit@K} is the
proportion of users with at least one relevant item in the top-$K$,
\begin{equation}
\text{Hit@K} = \frac{1}{|U|} \sum_{u \in U}
  \mathbb{I}\bigl(y_u \in \mathcal{R}_u^K\bigr);
\label{eq:hit}
\end{equation}
MRR captures the position of the first relevant item and nDCG@K weights relevant
items by position~\cite{jarvelin2002cumulated}. \textbf{Intra-List Diversity}
measures redundancy inside one list~\cite{ziegler2005improving},
\begin{equation}
\text{ILD} = \frac{2}{K(K-1)}\sum_{i<j} \bigl(1 -
  \cosine(\vect{e}_i, \vect{e}_j)\bigr),
\label{eq:ild}
\end{equation}
and \textbf{Emerging Artist Discovery} measures long-tail surfacing,
\begin{equation}
\text{EAD} = \frac{1}{K} \sum_{i=1}^{K}
  \mathbb{I}\bigl(\text{Listeners}(a_i) < \tau\bigr),
\qquad \tau = 10{,}000,
\label{eq:ead}
\end{equation}
the primary success indicator given our motivation. On the conversational side,
\textbf{SCS} measures alignment between the query and the recommended track with
BERT embeddings~\cite{devlin2019bert,gao2021advances},
\begin{equation}
\text{SCS} = \frac{1}{|U|}\sum_{u \in U}
  \cosine\bigl(\text{BERT}(q_u), \text{BERT}(m_{r_u})\bigr),
\label{eq:scs}
\end{equation}
\textbf{IAR} is the rate at which the top-1 result matches the explicit intent
stated in the dialogue, and \textbf{RC} is the BLEU-4 score of the generated
explanation against human-annotated references.

Table~\ref{tab:results} and Figure~\ref{fig:performance} support both
hypotheses. EchoPulse AI doubles the Hit@10 of the strongest sequential baseline
(0.312 vs.\ 0.164) while lifting ILD by 45.3\% and EAD by 157.9\% relative to
collaborative filtering. The diversity gain matters: by popularity tier,
emerging-artist recommendations rise from 12.7\% (collaborative filtering) and
20.1\% (TalkPlay) to 32.7\%, which is the effect this research was designed to
produce~\cite{brynjolfsson2011long,porcaro2021diversity}. Paired $t$-tests
confirm the ILD and EAD improvements are not random variation
($p<0.001$). Unlike re-ranking methods that inject diversity at the cost of
accuracy, our results show the two can be optimised jointly once the model is
grounded in intrinsic multimodal content rather than historical popularity
loops. The generative metrics point the same way: SCS 0.741 versus 0.623, IAR
0.698 versus 0.587 and RC 0.412 versus 0.358 BLEU-4 against the TalkPlay
baseline.

The ablation study attributes those gains to specific components: removing the
IntentTransformer costs 7.9\% of MRR, eliminating MST-MR costs 15.2\% of Hit@10,
and cross-attention alone contributes 14.1\% of nDCG@10, validating the decision
to let modalities attend to each other. On latency, core retrieval takes 125\,ms,
re-ranking 90\,ms and metadata enrichment 15\,ms (215\,ms combined, mean of 100
runs), whereas the two Qwen 2.5 stages add 2,800\,ms for keyword translation and
3,200\,ms for the final response, giving an end-to-end pipeline of about 6.23\,s.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Three findings stand out. Cross-attention beats early and late fusion, and every
encoder contributes information the others cannot recover, consistent with the
multimodal literature~\cite{baltruvsaitis2019multimodal}. The local lakehouse met
the reproducibility and traceability goals without cloud services, which matters
for research groups with limited budgets and unreliable connectivity. And the
157.9\% EAD gain suggests that algorithmic curation can balance user satisfaction
with ecosystem health~\cite{brynjolfsson2011long}: for musicians in Colombia,
where entering mainstream recommendation is nearly impossible, this is an
alternative pathway to an audience.

Relative to the baselines, the difference is structural rather than numerical.
BERT4Rec and SASRec process interaction sequences only, so they inherit
popularity bias; TalkPlay generates recommendations in language but without
structured multimodal fusion or generative-component evaluation. EchoPulse AI
replaces historical popularity signals with content affinity, which is precisely
what an artist without play history needs.

Limitations. The 5,710-track Master Table reflects the rigour of the
multi-dataset matching required to guarantee complete multimodal coverage, not a
bottleneck of the architecture: the fusion mechanism operates on
fixed-dimensional latent spaces, so inference scales linearly with catalogue
size and is amenable to approximate nearest neighbour indexing (for example
FAISS) for industrial deployment. Beyond scale, the system mainly recommends
English-language content, since WASABI, MSD and TalkPlayData-2 are predominantly
English, which limits its reach for Spanish-language music and for regional
Colombian genres such as vallenato, cumbia and bambuco. The matching pipeline is
specific to these four datasets, so adapting it to arbitrary catalogs needs more
flexible ingestion and automated schema mapping. The $\sim$6\,s end-to-end
latency, driven by the LLM stages, may hurt latency-sensitive use cases, although
caching and distillation could mitigate it. Finally, the evaluation is offline by
design: this stage establishes the mathematical and empirical validity of
multimodal fusion and bias mitigation, which is the prerequisite for the
resource-intensive online trials that come later.

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented EchoPulse AI, a locally deployed multimodal music recommender that
consolidates heterogeneous datasets through a five-stage local pipeline and fuses
lyrical, acoustic, metadata and conversational signals with cross-attention, so
that content affinity rather than historical popularity drives the ranking. On a
controlled 5,710-track Master Table it improved Intra-List Diversity by 45.3\%
and Emerging Artist Discovery by 157.9\% over collaborative filtering, while
keeping competitive accuracy (Hit@10\,=\,0.312) and reaching the best SCS, IAR
and RC among the compared systems. We plan to release the Master Table and the
TalkPlayData-2 processing scripts as a public benchmark for reproducible
multimodal music recommendation research.

Future work follows four directions: scaling to industrial-size catalogues with
ANN indexing while preserving multimodal integrity; controlled online A/B studies
to measure perceived quality and diversity satisfaction; multilingual adaptation
to Spanish lyrics and Latin American genres with encoders such as mBERT or XLM-R;
and a reinforcement-learning-from-human-feedback loop that updates
cross-attention weights from real-time corrections, together with per-modality
encoders and distillation for edge deployment. This is only a first step, but it
is a necessary one.
